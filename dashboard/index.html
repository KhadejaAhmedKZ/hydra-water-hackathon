RO, reverse osmosis

Seawater is pushed at high pressure through a membrane that lets water pass but holds back salt.

Cost of water

Every cost of building and running the plant, spread over each cubic meter of water it delivers. Net of solar export revenue.

Membrane life

How many years a membrane lasts before it has to be replaced.

Cycle tolerance

How many start/stop cycles a membrane survives before it must be replaced. At one start a day, 360 cycles is about one year.

Permanent damage per start/stop in warm, very salty seawater has not yet been measured, so this is the decisive unknown.

Biofouling

A living film of bacteria and algae that grows on the membrane and slows water through it. On this index, 1 is open ocean. Serious biofouling is documented at Gulf plants.

Tariff spread

How far grid electricity prices differ between expensive and cheap hours under a time-of-use tariff. 0 is today's flat UAE industrial tariff.

The three strategies

Solar, steady output. How Gulf plants run today.

Follow the sun, ignore wear. What dispatch models without membrane wear recommend.

Follow the sun + price wear. Our controller. Same options, but membrane damage has a price.

Reading the tipping point

Lines show the central case (biofouling index 2.0). Our controller tracks steady output until restarts become cheap enough, then follows the sun.

Break-even is reported to the nearest 100 cycles because grid values are rounded to 4 decimals.

Operating plants typically replace membranes after 2 to 5 years (Avlonitis et al. 2003).
